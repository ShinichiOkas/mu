# Plot Outline: The Clockwork Heart (仮題)

## 1. Theme
**Repair (修復)**
- Not just fixing a broken object, but the attempt to restore a lost state or a lost connection.
- The paradox of "perfect repair": Does fixing something make it new, or does it merely preserve the memory of the break?

## 2. Story Flow

### Introduction (The Setting)
- **Protagonist**: Elias, an elderly, meticulous master clockmaker/automaton repairman in a steampunk-esque city.
- **Setting**: A cluttered workshop filled with ticking gears and dormant brass figures.
- **Incident**: A young woman brings in a shattered, antique automaton—a small mechanical bird—that belonged to her late mother. It is beyond conventional repair; the core is crushed.

### Development (The Process of Repair)
- Elias takes on the challenge, not for money, but for the obsession of "complete restoration."
- He spends weeks sourcing rare parts, polishing gears, and delicately re-soldering connections.
- Through the process, the narrative focuses on the sensory details of repair: the smell of oil, the precision of tweezers, the frustration of a gear that won't click.
- Elias reflects on his own life—his loneliness and the things in his own heart he couldn't "repair."

### Climax (The Moment of Completion)
- Elias installs the final spring. He winds the key.
- The bird awakens. It doesn't just sing; it mimics a specific, haunting melody that the woman recognizes as her mother's favorite lullaby.
- The woman is overwhelmed with emotion, thanking Elias for "bringing back a piece of her mother."

### Conclusion (The Perception Reversal)
- The woman leaves. Elias sits back in his chair, exhausted.
- He reaches up to his own chest and opens a small hatch in his sternum.
- He removes a worn-out gear from his own heart and replaces it with a spare part he had kept.
- **The Reversal**: It is revealed that Elias himself is an automaton. The "master repairman" is a machine designed to repair other machines, perhaps programmed with the memories of a human clockmaker. His "loneliness" and "obsession" were part of a long-term maintenance cycle to ensure his own functionality through the act of repairing others.

## 3. Mechanism for 'Reversal of Perception' (認識の反転)
- **Initial Perception**: The reader perceives Elias as a human craftsman showing empathy and dedication toward a machine.
- **Reversal**: The final scene reveals that the "human" is actually the "machine." The act of repair was not just a service for a client, but a biological-like necessity for his own survival/maintenance. The empathy felt by the reader is shifted from "human kindness" to "the poignant tragedy of a machine mimicking humanity to sustain itself."
- **Key Reveal**: The physical action of Elias opening his own chest to perform self-maintenance.
